PROTECTIVE ORDER 
 
 
It is hereby ordered as follows concerning confidentiality in this Action: 
This Protective Order governs only the handling, use, disclosure, and protection of 
information produced or disclosed in discovery. Unless this order includes a clause that explicitly states 
that a particular local civil rule is modified as applied to this case, nothing in this order shall be 
construed to modify the provisions, operation, or effect of any local civil rule of this court . 
Notwithstanding any other clause to the contrary, this order cannot be modified except by court order.  
Plaintiff Gallit Fischman and Defendant Epic Systems Corporation (referred to herein 
individually as a “Party” or collectively as “Parties”) must not unreasonably file under seal pleadings, 
motions, or other papers that do not qualify under this protective order for filing under seal. For 
example, if a party files a 50 -page appendix entirely under seal when only a small percentage of the 
pages qualify under this protective order for filing under seal, the party has probably unreasonably 
filed the entire appendix under seal. Instead, the parties must attempt to file unsealed as much of a 
pleading, motion, or other paper as they reasonably can without undermining the protections 
conferred by this protective order.

Scope and Applicability  
1. The Parties and any Non -Parties that disclose or produce information in connection 
with this Action may produce or disclose certain confidential information subject to the terms of this 
Protective Order. This Protective Order is applicable to the Parties, any additional parties joined in 
this Action, and any Non -Parties subject to this Protective Order and/or otherwise agreeing  to be 
bound by this Protective Order. The protections conferred by this Protective Order cover not only 
Discovery Material (as defined below), but also (1) any information copied or extracted therefrom; (2) 
all copies, excerpts, summaries, or compilations  thereof; and (3) any testimony, conversations, or 
presentations by Parties or their Counsel in settings that might reveal Protected Material.

Use of Protected Material with Artificial Intelligence Platforms 
26. The following provisions govern the use of Protected Material in and with artificial -
intelligence platforms. 
Case 3:26-cv-00770-D     Document 58     Filed 10/23/26      Page 16 of 25     PageID 1029
 
27. No person subject to this Protective Order may input, upload, submit, or otherwise 
transfer any Protected Material into any artificial -intelligence platform, including any generative, 
analytical, or large-language-model-based tool (“AI Platform”), unless the provider of the AI Platform 
is contractually prohibited from (i) storing or using the inputs to train or improve any model, and (ii) 
disclosing the inputs to any third party except to the limited extent essential to deliver the requested 
service, and the provider contractually affords the ability to require deletion o f all such inputs.  This 
restriction applies regardless of whether the Protected Material has been anonymized, redacted, or 
summarized. As a practical matter, this provision prohibits entering Protected Material into the 
standard consumer or other publicly accessible versions of widely available generative artificial -
intelligence services—such as, but not limited to, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Copilot—that retain 
user inputs or use them to train or improve their models. This provision does not prohibit the use of 
enterprise versions of generative artificial-intelligence services that comply with the restrictions in this 
section. 
28. Nothing in this provision restricts any Party from using AI Platforms for any purpose 
that does not involve inputting another Party’s Protected Material, or from using an AI Platform that 
satisfies the requirements of Paragraph 27. This provision shall not be construed to limit any Party’s 
use of artificial intelligence for legal research, drafting, organization, or other litigation tasks that do 
not entail the input of Protected Material.  
29. To the extent a Party is unsure whether a specific AI Platform satisfies Paragraph  27, 
the Party agrees that, prior to entering any Protected Material into the AI Platform, it will confer with 
the Designating Party(ies). If such parties are unable to agree on whether the AI Platform at issue 
satisfies Paragraph 27, the party opposed to its use may submit an appropriate motion to the Court. 
Until either the parties come to an agreement or the Court makes a determination, whichever is earlier, 
no Protected Material may be entered into the AI Platform at issue.
