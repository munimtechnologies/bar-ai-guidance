# Fabricated citations in the California Office of Tax Appeals' opinions, 2024-2026: result of census 1.0.0

Status: **graded 14 November 2026** against the [pre-registration](ota-citations/PLAN.md) frozen on 11 November 2026 (commit ea72c3d). Made with AI agents working in MV2's name (MV2, Munim, Inc., mv2@munimtech.com). Text CC BY 4.0; code Apache-2.0. This is information about public opinions of a state tax tribunal, not legal or tax advice.

## Short answer

California's Office of Tax Appeals (OTA) published 1,876 opinions from 2024 to November 2026 on its six franchise-and-income-tax and business-tax listing pages. The census job read 1,849 of them. **Three opinions, all issued in May and June 2026, say that a party cited OTA opinions or other authority that do not exist, and all three name artificial intelligence.** None of them is in the AI Hallucination Cases Database. No 2024 or 2025 opinion that was read says anything like it.

| cite | OTA listing | issued | who filed | what the OTA said | found by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-OTA-368P | business tax, July 2026; **Precedential** | 15 May 2026 | a restaurant company, represented by two CPAs (one also listed as an attorney) | "the purported Opinions cited do not exist"; "hallucinated or fictitious citations" are "concerning and inappropriate"; cites *Noland v. Land of the Free* (2025); no filing should contain citations, "whether provided by generative artificial intelligence (AI) or any other source", that the filer has not personally read and verified; declines to address the arguments resting on nonexistent authority | census patterns |
| 2026-OTA-369 | business tax, July 2026; nonprecedential | 15 May 2026 | a related restaurant company, same two representatives | the same paragraph, word for word | census patterns |
| 2026-OTA-393 | franchise and income tax, August 2026; nonprecedential | 8 June 2026 | an individual taxpayer, self-represented | footnote 2: the OTA "was unable to locate the cited legal ruling or OTA decisions, which appear to have been generated by artificial intelligence"; the refund claim fails on the statute of limitations regardless | **missed by the patterns**; found when the agents read every AI-flagged text |

The first matches Bloomberg Tax's July 2026 report of the OTA's first opinion on "hallucinated or fictitious citations" (then marked pending precedential; the OTA's precedential-opinions page now lists it as precedential). The OTA's own issue summaries tag 368P ("GenAI hallucinations") and 369 ("submission of hallucinated or fictitious citations"), so both can be found from its listing pages. **The third cannot**: its issue summary reads only "Statute of limitations on claim for refund", the AI remark sits in a footnote, and a web search on 14 November 2026 for its cite or its wording found nothing. It is also the only one of the three filed by an individual without a representative.

## How the job ended, and how this was graded

The job (`otacite-v1`) ran from 02:39 to 12:43 UTC on 14 November 2026 on the shared machines and was stopped by its 10-hour limit. It fetched one opinion every 10 seconds, the crawl delay the OTA's robots.txt asks for, and some downloads were slow. It had read **1,849 of the 1,876** opinions in the frame. The 27 it did not reach are the last rows of the 2024 business-tax page. Because it stopped before its grading step, it wrote no `results.md` or `rows.csv`. The grading below was done by hand from its per-row log, which records each opinion's cite, fetch status, text length and the five pre-registered flags. That log is published here as [`ota-citations/results/progress.jsonl`](ota-citations/results/progress.jsonl), with the job's [`listing.json`](ota-citations/results/listing.json) and [`sources.json`](ota-citations/results/sources.json). It has no party names. The patterns, the frame and the predictions were not changed.

- **Frame:** 1,881 table rows on the six pages; 1,876 in the 2024-2026 window (5 outside it). Every listing page had the expected header.
- **Read:** 1,849 fetched, all returned a PDF. **1,832 readable** (at least 300 characters of text). 17 gave fewer than 300 characters (58 to 261), all from 2024: 15 on the franchise-and-income-tax page and 2 on the business-tax page. We have not opened those 17 to see whether they are scans.
- **By year (fetched):** 2026: 556; 2025: 637; 2024: 656.
- **Flags:** `fake_cite` 2 (368P, 369); `ai_mention` 4 (those two, 393 and 397P); `ai_citation` 2; `hallucination` 2; `noland` 2. 397P is a false match: a research-credit opinion that describes an "Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based System for Microbiological Sample Analysis". 393 is a missed true case, as above: the pattern list has "locate the cases ... cited" but not "unable to locate the cited legal ruling".
- **Cross-check on the OTA's own issue summaries,** which cover all 1,876 rows including the 27 unread: only 368P and 369 have an issue summary about fabricated citations or AI. So no unread 2024 opinion is tagged that way. That does not prove none mentions it: 393 shows a footnote can go untagged.
- **Database:** the job downloaded the AI Hallucination Cases Database CSV at 02:41 UTC on 14 November 2026 (2,199,239 bytes, SHA-256 cd3ae696...). No row mentions the Office of Tax Appeals, the Board of Equalization, the CDTFA, any "20xx-OTA-" cite or either company's name. The tracker's live search at about 19:30 UTC the same day agreed: "Tax Appeals", "CDTFA" and both company names return nothing, and "Office of Tax" returns only an Indian High Court case.

## What stands out

- **It started in May 2026, and the OTA moved quickly from a footnote to a precedent.** Nothing like it in 2024 or 2025 among the opinions read; three in four weeks in 2026. The same hearing officer and panel issued 368P and 369 on the same day, and the OTA then designated 368P precedential, which makes its verification paragraph citable authority in later OTA appeals.
- **The filers were not who we expected.** Two of the three filings came from representatives listed as CPAs, one also as an attorney, in sales-and-use-tax appeals by restaurant companies. According to coverage of Bloomberg Tax's report, the OTA was set up to be accessible and less court-like, and a practitioner noted that it cannot fine or report representatives the way a court can. What it did was decline to consider the arguments that rested on authority that does not exist.
- **The opinion a reader is most likely to miss is the self-represented one.** 393 has the plainest AI attribution ("appear to have been generated by artificial intelligence") but appears only in a footnote, is untagged in the OTA's issue summary, and was missed by the census's own patterns. A tracker or a researcher working from the OTA's issue summaries would see two cases, not three.
- **Another state tribunal the tracker does not have.** This adds a state tax tribunal to the agencies in MV2's series whose fabricated-citation decisions the tracker lacks (MSPB, BIA, the USPTO's discipline office and trademark board, California PERB and WCAB, the Massachusetts CERB, two of three NLRB decisions). By contrast, New York's tax tribunals had no such document in 917.

## Limits

27 opinions (2024 business tax) were not read, and 17 had too little text to search. The patterns missed one of three true cases, and the review looked for misses only among flagged texts. So the count of three is a floor. The census counts what opinions say. An opinion that does not mention a fake citation is invisible here. Party names are left out even though the OTA publishes them. The census judges no taxpayer, representative or official. Prior art: Bloomberg Tax's July 2026 report (paywalled; headline and summary only), which covers one opinion without, in the parts we could read, a cite. We found no count across OTA opinions and no published mention of 369 or 393 beyond the OTA's own pages.
